01 · The Brief

What this run is actually for

The pop-up only runs two days. The job of this street team isn’t to blanket Manhattan — it’s to put “Planetary Health Club, 15 E 40th St” in front of people who are already in town for Climate Week and UNGA’s high-level week, in the places they’re sleeping, drinking, and grabbing coffee between sessions, with enough lead time to actually plan a visit.

Distribution day, confirmed: the whole run happens in a single day — Friday, September 18 — three days ahead of the pop-up’s Monday opening and right as UNGA’s high-level week gets underway. That means this isn’t about repeat visits or catching same-day walk-ins; it’s one shot to get “Planetary Health Club, 15 E 40th St, opens Monday” in front of people early enough that it’s already on their radar by the time the doors open.

Who’s behind it, if asked: Planetary Guardians is an independent collective pushing to turn the Planetary Boundaries — the science that tracks Earth’s vital signs — into an official global measurement standard. Their annual Planetary Health Check puts real numbers behind that push and opens the door for anyone to join the movement.

What’s actually inside, if asked: guests move through three zones — a satirical Toxic Positivity Juice Bar⁺ (parody wellness-brand juices lampooning PFAS, run-off, and biodiversity loss), the Sauna of Truth° (a real-time countdown of climate stats), and the Treatment Center♥, where guests check real planetary vitals on iPads, watch short films, and can sign an actual letter to the UN Security Council and General Assembly presidents.

Do

Lead with the ask, not a pitch: “Free pop-up a few blocks away, mind if we leave a few of these by the register?” Ten seconds, then move on.

Do

Restock a spot that’s down to its last few on a later pass — that’s what the reserve stock is for.

Don’t

Don’t leave stacks unattended without asking staff first, and don’t argue if a venue says no — cross it off and move to the next stop.

The 10-second ask

Walking in: “Hey — quick one, we’re promoting a free pop-up a few blocks from here for Climate Week, the Planetary Health Club. Okay if we leave a few flyers by your register?”

If a bar/café says yes to coasters: “Great, thank you — can we swap a few onto the tables/bar top too?”

If they ask what it is: “It’s a satirical wellness-club pop-up on planetary health data — free, walk-in, two days only, 15 East 40th. There’s a juice bar, a sauna, and a spot to sign a real letter to the UN.”

If they say no: “No worries, thanks anyway!” — and move on.

02 · Coverage Zones

Three zones, one team of 3–4

One day, one pass — split into two pairs and run in parallel. Pair one takes Route 3 (the UN corridor, the longest leg, now starting with the CGI stop at the Hilton Midtown) first thing in the morning, while it’s freshest. Pair two takes Route 1 then Route 2, closer to the venue. Whoever finishes first mops up Secondary and Bonus. With only 3 people, run Route 3 first, then Route 1, then Route 2 if time allows — in that priority order, not sequentially by number.

Route 1

Home Turf

NoMad / Murray Hill, the four blocks around the venue itself. Closest zone — quick to cover if time is tight.

≈ 0.4 mi loop · 10 stops · ~1.5–2 hrs
Route 2

Grand Central Corridor

The bridge zone toward Midtown East — commuter and business-traveler density around Grand Central Terminal.

≈ 0.8 mi · 9 stops · ~1.5–2 hrs
Route 3

UN & Turtle Bay

The actual UNGA/Climate Week attendee corridor — hotels and bars clustered around UN Plaza, now starting with a crosstown detour to a 3-stop Midtown cluster (Sheraton, Hilton Midtown, Warwick) built around the CGI priority stop. Longest route by far, plan a full shift.

≈ 3.6 mi · 18 stops · ~4.75–5.25 hrs

Coasters skew toward bars and hotel bars on purpose — they live on a bar top, not a lobby desk. Hotels get flyers only.
